# Bank Customer Churn Prediction

## STA332 Group Research Project | University of the Western Cape | 2026

### Project Overview

This project investigates factors associated with customer churn in a retail banking environment. Using a sample of 1,000 bank customers from France, Germany and Spain, the study examines whether holding a high number of bank products (three or more) indicates customer dissatisfaction and increased churn risk rather than customer loyalty.

The analysis uses statistical techniques implemented in **SAS** to identify customer characteristics associated with the likelihood of leaving the bank.

### Research Question

Does holding a high number of bank products (3 or more) signal customer dissatisfaction and elevated churn risk, rather than loyalty, once other factors are controlled for?

### Dataset

The study uses a sample of 1,000 customers from a retail banking dataset covering three European countries:

- France
- Germany
- Spain

The response variable is **Exited**, which indicates whether a customer left the bank.

The explanatory variables include:

- Credit Score
- Geography
- Gender
- Age
- Tenure
- Account Balance
- Number of Products
- Credit Card Ownership
- Active Membership
- Estimated Salary

### Statistical Methods and Tools

The analysis was conducted using **SAS** and included:

- Data cleaning and preparation
- Descriptive statistical analysis
- Exploratory data analysis and graphical visualisation
- Correlation analysis
- Chi-square tests of association
- Simple and multiple logistic regression
- Forward, backward and stepwise variable selection
- ROC curve analysis and model evaluation
- Classification analysis

### Key Findings

The research identified several factors associated with customer churn:

- Customers holding three or more bank products showed substantially higher churn rates.
- Inactive members were more likely to leave the bank.
- Customers in Germany exhibited higher churn compared with the other geographical groups.
- Age, tenure, geography, gender, number of products and active membership were important variables in the reduced logistic regression model.

The findings highlight the importance of understanding customer behaviour and product usage when developing customer retention strategies.
